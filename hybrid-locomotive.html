02

DYNAMIC BRAKING, STEP BY STEP

throw the brake in the cab above - the sequence lights up in sync
LIVE CIRCUIT MAP · CONTACTOR FOLLOWS THE CAB · BATTERY LINE SHOWS BLEND FLOW
  1. 1

    CUT THE LINE

    Pulling the brake handle throws the main contactor: the engine's generator opens from the traction bus. The motor loses its supply, but it is still spinning at wheel speed.

  2. 2

    MOTOR BECOMES A GENERATOR

    Any armature spinning faster than its applied voltage wants to feed current, not drink it. With the bus disconnected, that current has nowhere to go until the contactor gives it somewhere.

  3. 3

    CURRENT RUNS BACKWARDS

    The contactor lands on the grid contact. Back-EMF now drives current out of the motor, up the roof feed line, into the resistor bank. Electromagnetic counter-torque on the axles is the braking force itself.

  4. 4

    ENERGY BECOMES HEAT

    Up to 480 kW of kinetic energy is dumped into the fins - watch them climb from steel-grey to cherry glow and shimmer off the roof. At 880 C the circuit auto-releases to protect the grid.

03

WHERE THE ENERGY GOES

no magic - conservation of energy, on a roof
KINETIC1/2 m·v² - the train's own motion and momentum
RESISTOR GRIDI²R loss - up to 480 kW of hot copper fins
HEATradiated and fanned away into the night air

When you pull the dynamic brake, the locomotive is not spending stored power at all - it is spending the train's own momentum. The motors flip into generators, dump current backwards into the roof grid, and the kinetic energy leaves the system as heat. No friction, no worn brake shoes - just counter-torque and a roof that gets hot enough to glow.

SO WHY THE BATTERY?

The 340 kWh pack smooths engine load, carries the cab's auxiliaries and starts the prime mover - and with the source-blend handle it can carry any share of the traction load, from a 50/50 split to full electric. What it is not, is the brake: a pack sized to swallow 480 kW of braking current every single stop would be a different (and far pricier) machine. Classic dynamic braking accepts the waste heat, because heat is free, reliable and infinitely repeatable.
